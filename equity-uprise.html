Policy desk

Make the rule measurable.

The current infrastructure work treats ordinance language as an operating system: triggers, evidence, review, enforcement, cure and appeal.

01 · Classification

Regulate the project that exists, not the label on the application.

Use electrical demand and physical scale together. Aggregate phases, buildings, common-control entities, contiguous tracts and shared infrastructure so one campus cannot be sliced into smaller applications to escape review.

Policy principle Thresholds are policy choices. Anti-segmentation is an enforcement necessity.

02 · Acoustics

Low-frequency sound needs its own protocol.

Baseline conditions, repeatable measurement positions, ten-minute LAeq, one-third-octave analysis and tonality review create an enforceable record that ordinary A-weighted limits can miss.

Policy principle Write the measurement method into the rule before the complaint arrives.

03 · Water

Capacity is not a brochure number.

Separate metering, watershed capacity certification, Water Usage Effectiveness reporting, cooling-method disclosure and drought response connect local approvals to real system constraints.

Policy principle Routine reporting turns a promise into an operating condition.

04 · Power

Distinguish reliability from routine generation.

Electrical load, interconnection assumptions, emergency backup, peak shaving and routine onsite generation create different grid, air-quality and neighborhood effects. The ordinance should not collapse them into one box.

Policy principle Define what may operate, when, for how long and under which permit.

05 · Review

Independent expertise needs governance too.

County-selected reviewers, qualification standards, conflict rules, applicant-funded escrow, scoped deliverables, invoices and an applicant response process prevent “independent review” from becoming another vague discretion point.

Policy principle The reviewer should be independent of both the applicant and the political mood.

06 · Compliance

The permit is not the finish line.

Commissioning, operational verification, periodic reporting, corrective action, notice and cure, emergency authority, retesting, written determinations and appeal create a system that still works years after approval.

Policy principle Continuing compliance is what converts siting policy into operating policy.
