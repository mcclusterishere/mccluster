1 · What we collect

What you give us

  • An account. Email address and password. The password is hashed by our authentication provider and is never visible to us.
  • Your details, if you fill them in. Name, postal address, phone, and the year you were born. All optional. The birth year exists because sharing a minor's details is a different legal question from sharing an adult's, and the answer has to be recorded before the ask.
  • What you post. On Mnet: posts, replies, follows, blocks, mutes, your profile photo and anything you attach.
  • Purchases. What you bought and the email it is tied to. Card numbers never reach us — Stripe handles payment and we are told only that it succeeded.
  • Messages you send us. Enquiries, licensing requests, fellowship applications.

What your device sends

Every request to any website carries these. We record them rather than pretending they are not there:

IP addressAbuse prevention, and to derive rough location. Recorded server-side.

Your network (the ISP or carrier your connection belongs to) To tell a real listener from a datacentre, and to spot abuse.

What you do here

Which pages you open, which tracks you play and for how long, what you save, search and download, and how far down a page you read. This is what ranks the listening room — the ordering is computed from real listening, not bought.

What we do not collect, and will not

  • No Google Analytics. No advertising pixels. No Meta pixel, no TikTok pixel.
  • No cross-site tracking. Nothing we set is readable by any other website.
  • No keystrokes, and no reading of what you type. A copy from a page is recorded as having happened; the words are not banked. Anything marked as your own record — your address, your phone — is exempt even from that.
  • No microphone, no camera, no contacts. No precise device location — we never ask your browser or phone for GPS, and the only location we have is the rough one an IP address implies, which is usually your city and is sometimes wrong by a hundred miles.
  • Nothing is sold. Ever. Not to advertisers, not to data brokers, not to anyone. There is no "trusted partners" clause here because there are none.

3 · What is public on Mnet

A social network only works if it is clear what everyone can see. On Mnet:

  • Public: your display name, your photo, your posts and replies, and who you follow.
  • Only you: your email, your postal address, your phone, your birth year, your purchases, and anything you blocked or muted.
  • Blocks and mutes are not announced. The person is not told.

A removed post is kept as a record of the removal, with the reason, rather than being erased from the account of what was moderated and why.

5 · How the services work together

One McCluster account signs you into all of it — the site, Mnet, Equity Uprise. Your identity, follows and profile travel with you, because they are one system rather than several pretending to be.

What does not travel: a fellowship application does not become marketing, and a purchase does not put you on the mailing list. The mailing list has exactly one door in, and it is the box you tick yourself.

6 · Your rights, and how to use them

Depending on where you live — Connecticut, the rest of the US, the UK, the EU — you have some or all of these. We extend all of them to everyone, because running two standards is how you get one of them wrong:

  • Know what we hold about you.
  • Get a copy of it.
  • Correct it.
  • Delete it, including your whole account.
  • Withdraw consent at any time, as easily as you gave it.
  • Opt out of sale or targeted advertising — trivially, since we do neither.
  • Not be discriminated against for exercising any of it.

Email matthew@mccluster.org and say what you want. A person reads it. We answer within 45 days and usually the same week. We will not make you create an account to ask.

Global Privacy Control is honoured. If your browser sends GPC — or the older Do Not Track — we stop recording your IP, your device identifier, your session, and your city and coordinates, automatically and with nothing to click. Country and timezone survive so the counts stay honest.

9 · Legal requests and preventing harm

We will hand over information if the law genuinely requires it. We will read the request first, refuse one that overreaches, and tell you it happened unless we are legally forbidden from doing so.

We may also act without a request to stop fraud, abuse, or a credible threat to someone's safety.

10 · If you are a business customer

Different relationship, so it is set out separately.

Where we host your mailboxes, domain or site, the contents are yours. We are the processor and you are the controller. We hold what is technically necessary to deliver it: your mail in storage, the logs that prove delivery, and your account and billing records.

We do not read your mail. Spam filtering runs on our own server and no third party sees the contents. We do not scan it for advertising — there is no advertising — and we do not train anything on it.

Access happens only when you ask for support, or where the law requires it. Either way it is logged.

If you leave, your data leaves with you: we will export it, and delete our copies within 30 days of your asking.

Analytics on sites we build

Sites we build and host carry McCluster Insight, our own first-party analytics. It records the same things described in section 1 — pages, clicks, scroll depth, device type, rough location — for that site, so its owner can see how it is performing. It replaces Google Analytics rather than adding to it.

  • It is first-party. What it stores lives on that site’s own domain and is readable by no other website, including ours.
  • It honours Global Privacy Control and Do Not Track before sending anything — checked in the browser and again on our server.
  • It is not an advertising pixel. It feeds no ad network, and nothing it collects is sold or shared with anyone outside the site’s owner and us.

When activity is linked across sites, and when it is not. If a site uses M Account sign-in, a signed-in person’s activity there can be joined to their activity elsewhere in the McCluster ecosystem, because it is the same account they chose to sign in with. A visitor who is not signed in is counted on the site they are on and nowhere else — we do not attempt to recognise them across domains by fingerprint, by IP, or by any other means.

If you run a site we host, your own privacy notice has to say this too — the visitors are yours, and the disclosure obligation is yours as well as ours. Ask and we will send you the paragraph to paste, kept in step with this page.

11 · Children

This service is not for under-13s and we do not knowingly hold their information. Some things — sharing details onward — are offered only to adults, which is why the birth year is asked. Tell us if a child has made an account and it will be removed.

12 · Changes, and how you will know

The date at the top changes when this does. For a change that materially affects what we collect or who sees it, we will tell you before it takes effect — by email if you are on the list, and on the site if you are not. We will not widen what we collect and let you find out by reading this page again.
