What this site
records about you.

The whole list, in plain English, including the parts most sites leave vague. This site runs no Google Analytics, no advertising pixel and no third-party tracker of any kind. What it measures, it measures itself, and keeps to itself.

What the server sees

Every request to every website on the internet arrives carrying an address, because that is how a reply finds its way back. This one writes it down:

  • Your IP address.
  • An approximate location worked out from that address — country, region, city, postal code, an approximate latitude and longitude, and a timezone. This is derived from the network, not from your device's location services, and it is accurate to roughly a city. It is not your street.
  • The network you are on — its operator's name and number, which is usually your internet provider or mobile carrier.
  • Your browser's user-agent string, and whether the request looks automated.

These are observed on arrival, not asked for. A browser cannot be asked for its own address — there is no such thing — which is precisely why the recording happens on the server instead of in the page.

What your browser volunteers

Alongside that, the page notes what the device can say about itself: screen size and colour depth, orientation, graphics hardware, touch support, language preferences, dark-mode and reduced-motion settings, whether cookies are on, battery level and charging state, and the speed of the connection. This is how the site is kept working on real phones rather than on a developer's desk.

How a visit is counted

  • A random identifier stored in your browser, so a return visit can be told from a first one. It is a random number and nothing else. Clearing your browser data genuinely erases it, and the next visit is a stranger.
  • A session identifier for one sitting, which lapses after thirty minutes of nothing.
  • If you are signed in, your account, verified against the login server.

Deliberately not done: there is no canvas, audio or font fingerprinting here. Those techniques exist specifically to recognise somebody who has cleared their data, which makes them a tracking measure rather than a counting one. The identifier this site uses is one you can actually get rid of.

What you do while you are here

Which pages you open and for how long; how far down you read and which sections held your attention; what you click, and the clicks that indicate the page is fighting you — a button tapped repeatedly, or one that did nothing. Errors that only ever happen to visitors. How quickly the page actually loaded on your device. When a form is started, which fields were visited in which order, how long each took and how often it was corrected. That a selection was copied, and how long it was.

What is never recorded

  • Nothing you type. Form measurement records field names, order and timing. The characters are never read, ever.
  • Password, payment and one-time-code fields are not instrumented at all, not even for timing.
  • Text you copy on a signed-in page, or out of any field, is not kept — only the fact that a copy happened and its length. What is on screen there is your information, not this site's.
  • No microphone, no camera, no clipboard reading, no location from your device's own location services.

Who it is shared with

Nobody. This data is not sold, not rented, not traded, and not handed to advertisers, data brokers or analytics companies — because there are none involved. It lives on infrastructure this site runs, is readable only by the site's owner, and is used to make the thing work better and to understand what people came for. It would be disclosed only where the law requires it.

How long it is kept

Behavioural records are retained for up to 400 days and then purged. Ask sooner and it goes sooner.

Your rights, and how to use them

Wherever you live, and regardless of whether your state or country has a law that grants them, you can ask for any of the following and it will be honoured:

  • See it — a copy of what is held about you.
  • Correct it if something is wrong.
  • Delete it — all of it.
  • Take it with you in a portable format.
  • Object to the measurement entirely.

Write to matthew@mccluster.org and say which. No account or special form is needed; if you are not signed in, say roughly when and from where you visited so the right records can be found. A reply comes within 45 days. If a request is refused you may appeal by replying, and that appeal gets a written answer.

Connecticut residents may also contact the Office of the Attorney General. There is no sale of personal data and no targeted advertising to opt out of here, because neither happens.

Turning it off

The site honours Global Privacy Control and the browser's Do Not Track setting where they are sent. Blocking the measurement entirely with any content blocker is fine; nothing on the site depends on it, and nothing will be withheld from you for it.
