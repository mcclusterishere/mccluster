A / AUDITORY → BIOLOGICAL

Listening and physiology

Music is processed by auditory, cognitive, and emotional systems. Small human transcriptomic studies have observed blood gene-expression or microRNA changes following listening or music performance. They do not establish gene-by-song activation, and effects vary across participants and study designs.

Kanduri et al., 2015 ↗
B / MECHANICAL → CELLULAR

Mechanotransduction

Mechanical forces can influence cells, while DNA itself undergoes molecular-scale dynamics. Audible music, ultrasound, and molecular vibrational modes occupy different experimental regimes. Our visual simulator is a square membrane—not a model of DNA being directly shaken by music.

Kumeta et al., 2018 ↗

Why not sort by genre or mood?

A frequency alone doesn't determine a cymatic shape. The resonator's geometry, support, damping, and excitation location matter, so we fix those first. We then organize tracks using measured frequency content, temporal variation, modal dominance, and spectral texture. Genre remains optional descriptive metadata—not a scientific prediction of a specific shape or of a listener's personality.

What the player learns—and refuses to assume

Playback chosen by a listener must be distinguished from a platform-selected song. Autoplay exposures do not automatically mean preference. No genetic trait, psychological diagnosis, cognitive ability, or medical condition is inferred from listening history. Research participation and any future long-term profile require separate affirmative consent.

REPRODUCIBLE SOURCES / READ THE RESEARCH

Evidence and methods

The model uses fixed-edge membrane eigenfunctions φmn(x,y) = sin(mπx/L)sin(nπy/L), with natural frequencies fmn = c/(2L)√(m²+n²). The sound's measured spectral energy drives a damped-response approximation, normalized for display. Relative amplitudes are not calibrated physical displacement and phases are not recovered from a magnitude-only audio spectrum.
