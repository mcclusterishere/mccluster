# THE STOCKSY SET: a dedicated body of work, shot to be let in

This is the shot list for the premium application the gates doc points
at (docs/the-vault-gates.md). It is **not** the assignment archive and it
never touches the microstock or editorial lanes. That is the whole
point of it.

## The law of this set, before the first frame

1. **Exclusive means exclusive.** Anything placed with Stocksy cannot be
   licensed through any other agency: not Adobe, not Shutterstock, not
   Alamy. The system encodes this as a `conflict` gate. These frames get
   their own folder, their own catalog prefix, and they never ride an
   FTP push to anyone else. Prints and your own portfolio stay fine.
2. **Every frame is released.** Model release for every recognizable
   person (guardian-signed for minors), property release for interiors
   that read as someone's business. The release is photographed, filed
   in the vault bucket, and rowed in `vault_releases` **before** the
   frame is eligible for the submission set. No paper, no submission.
   That's the same law the print shop now runs on (`rights: "released"`).
3. **25 minimum, 40 ceiling.** Stocksy asks for at least 25 images.
   Shoot the whole list, then submit the strongest 25 to 35 that read as
   one photographer with one point of view. Coherence is a stated
   acceptance criterion.
4. **The angle is the application.** Stocksy says outright they are
   over-supplied in landscape, nature, wildlife and weddings, and that
   applicants working *outside* those areas are more likely to get in.
   This set is authentic Black civic, family, and working life in metro
   Atlanta and Bridgeport: real people, real rooms, no synthetic
   anything. That is the strongest angle an applicant can carry.

## Craft rules that ride every shot

- Real collaborators, paid, never posed stiff. Direct activity, not
  smiles at camera. The frame should look overheard.
- Natural or motivated light. One consistent grade across the set.
- **No visible brands, logos, or trade dress.** Check shirts, cups,
  signage, screens. A logo kills a commercial frame.
- Shoot every scene horizontal AND vertical, plus one wide with clean
  negative space (buyers put copy there).
- Faces sharp, ISO clean, no heavy crush. They reject on technicals
  before they ever judge the eye.

## The series

### S1 · The Chair (Black-owned barbershop & beauty shop) · 7 frames
The interiors buyers search for and can't find released.
1. Barber mid-fade, client draped, morning window light, over-shoulder.
2. The mirror row: barber and client laughing in reflection.
3. Beautician sectioning hair, hands in sharp focus, face soft.
4. The waiting bench: three generations waiting, phones and paper.
5. Owner at the register, card reader out, genuine transaction energy.
6. Wide of the whole shop working, negative space in the ceiling line.
7. Detail: clippers, oils, the license framed on the wall (name masked).
*Buyer searches: black-owned business, barbershop, small business owner,
community, self-care.*

### S2 · The Table (multigenerational family life) · 7 frames
8. Sunday dinner being landed on the table: hands, steam, motion.
9. Grandmother teaching a recipe, kid on a stool watching the pot.
10. The porch: three generations, golden hour, nobody looking at camera.
11. Board game or spades mid-slam, real laughter.
12. Homework at the kitchen table, parent leaning in.
13. Family prayer or grace, heads bowed, shot from the doorway.
14. Leaving-for-church tableau at the front door, wide.
*Buyer searches: black family, multigenerational, family dinner, home,
authentic family moments.*

### S3 · The Owners (small business portraits) · 7 frames
Environmental portraits of real proprietors in their rooms: florist,

16. Hands doing the work: arranging, plating, folding, racking.
17. The open sign flip / roll-up door lift, start-of-day energy.

19. The delivery moment: box handed across a counter.
20. End of day: counting out, sweeping, lights half down.
21. Wide establishing of the block the businesses share.
*Buyer searches: entrepreneur, small business, black-owned, shop local,
mentorship at work.*

## What stays OUT of this set

- Anything from a client wall (eu-rally, candler, brunch-and-beats,
  dressed-for-success). Those are unreleased, and already non-exclusive
  property of the editorial/print lanes.
- Anything with a minor and no guardian signature in the vault.
- Anything AI-touched. Stocksy's position is 100% real; so is the pitch.

## The pipeline, when the set exists

1. Each frame gets a catalog id under a `stocksy-` prefix and a
   `vault_releases` row per person and property. `rights: "released"`.
2. Vault keyword pass (the Gemini pass), then a human read. Captions in
   plain sentences, no keyword stuffing.
3. Pick the 25 to 35 that grade and sequence as one voice. Kill darlings
   that break the look.
4. Apply. If Stocksy passes, this exact set (released, coherent,
   authored) is the Westend61 / Cavan application unchanged. They're
   non-exclusive; the conflict gate only locks if Stocksy says yes.
