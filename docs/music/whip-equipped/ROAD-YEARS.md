## I. The hundred-dollar camper

*Earlier. On the way to Georgia. The precise origin and date are not established. The following roadside stop and phone conversation are dramatic staging.*

*A tow vehicle waits beside a fuel pump. Behind it sits the camper McCluster bought for a hundred dollars. MAJID'S BROTHER looks from the camper to McCluster, who is looking at the pump's climbing total as though staring could slow it.*

**BROTHER:** Tell me what you paid for it again.

**MCCLUSTER:** A hundred.

**BROTHER:** And what did you pay me to get it down there?

**MCCLUSTER:** You remember what you charged me.

**BROTHER:** Six hundred. I just like hearing the two numbers together.

**MCCLUSTER:** You're enjoying this too much.

**BROTHER:** I'm towing your hundred-dollar house across the map. I have to find something to enjoy.

*McCluster walks back to the camper and looks through its doorway. Away from the pump, it becomes a bargain again. The little space inside is somewhere a person could go instead of staying where they are.*

**MCCLUSTER:** Somebody's going to want this.

**BROTHER:** Good. Ask them if they want to buy the gas too.

*McCluster calls Majid. He puts him on speaker, then immediately regrets making the conversation available to both brothers.*

**MAJID:** Where are you?

**MCCLUSTER:** Getting gas.

**MAJID:** Again?

**BROTHER:** Tell him about his investment.

**MCCLUSTER:** Everybody knows the investment. I spent a hundred dollars.

**MAJID:** You spent a hundred on the camper.

**MCCLUSTER:** Here we go.

**MAJID:** You've got my brother, the fuel, the whole trip. What does it owe you when you arrive?

**MCCLUSTER:** About a thousand. Depending on how much more this thing drinks.

**BROTHER:** Now he's selling a thousand-dollar camper. It grew up on the highway.

*McCluster laughs despite himself. The pump stops. He looks back at the total, then at the camper.*

**MCCLUSTER:** I can still make something. It doesn't need to be a miracle.

**MAJID:** That's what I'm asking. Can the deal work with the trip in it?

**MCCLUSTER:** Yes.

**MAJID:** Then stop introducing it to everybody as the hundred-dollar camper.

**MCCLUSTER:** That's the best part of the story.

**BROTHER:** It is. He's keeping that part.

*The brothers laugh. McCluster hangs up on the phone version of the laughter, but the other one is still standing beside him.*

*Later, in Georgia. A prospective buyer steps inside the camper. McCluster waits outside, trying to leave enough room for the person to imagine owning it. His impulse is to keep selling. For once, he lets the buyer look.*

**BUYER:** You brought this all the way down here?

**MCCLUSTER:** Had help bringing it.

**BUYER:** That's a lot of road for a sale.

**MCCLUSTER:** It was a lot of road before it was a sale.

**BUYER:** And you're asking twenty-five hundred?

**MCCLUSTER:** That's what we're talking about.

*The buyer goes back inside. McCluster takes his phone out, sees Majid's name, and puts it away without answering. He can call back when there is something to say.*

*After the sale, the space where the camper stood is empty. The remembered sale amount is approximately $2,500; the scene does not display a receipt or establish exact net profit.*

**MAJID — PHONE:** You sold it?

**MCCLUSTER:** Sold it.

**MAJID:** You going to tell me the number, or are you going to make me listen to the whole trip again?

**MCCLUSTER:** About twenty-five hundred.

**MAJID:** There you go.

*For a moment, neither of them needs the joke. McCluster looks at the empty space. Moving the camper had seemed like the entire problem. Now he has money from having moved it, and no camper left to think about.*

**MCCLUSTER:** I could do another one.

**MAJID:** I knew that was coming.

**MCCLUSTER:** Something I can drive this time.

**MAJID:** Call me before you buy it.

**MCCLUSTER:** Before I buy it, or before I call your brother?

**MAJID:** Before there's another house following us to Georgia.

*McCluster laughs and opens the listings.*

## II. The G20: a bedroom with a sale price

### Michigan

*Around 2024, in the period McCluster remembers as summertime. The G20 waits in Michigan. The flight cost about a hundred dollars; the van cost about eight hundred. AAA towing also belongs to the recollection, but the reason, leg, and cost are unresolved and are not invented here.*

*McCluster climbs into the rear. A sleeping space is already there. He sits on it, then stretches out with his shoes still on. He has bought something he can sell. He has also bought somewhere to put his body down.*

**MAJID — PHONE:** You looking at it now?

**MCCLUSTER:** I'm in it.

**MAJID:** How is it?

**MCCLUSTER:** Smooth. It has the sleeper and everything.

**MAJID:** You bought it already.

**MCCLUSTER:** Eight hundred out the door.

**MAJID:** That's not what I asked.

**MCCLUSTER:** You didn't ask a question. You made an observation.

*Majid laughs. McCluster turns onto his side and aims the phone around the little sleeping area as though giving a tour of an apartment.*

**MCCLUSTER:** Look. I'm serious. It's ready.

**MAJID:** I see it. Where are you staying tonight?

**MCCLUSTER:** I just showed you the room.

**MAJID:** You got money to get down there?

*McCluster lowers the phone. That question does not fit the tour.*

**MCCLUSTER:** I got the van. I got down here—up here. Wherever Michigan is in relation to my money right now.

**MAJID:** How much gas can you buy?

**MCCLUSTER:** Enough to start. I'll work on the way.

**MAJID:** Call me when you stop.

**MCCLUSTER:** I will.

**MAJID:** And let me see outside before you start telling everybody you bought a hotel for eight hundred dollars.

*McCluster climbs out and sends him a view of the van. There is genuine pride in the way he steps back to fit the whole thing into the frame.*

### Enough for the next stretch

*The southbound trip. The remembered road/living period is approximately four days; this sequence does not assign four days to every leg or establish an exact holding period.*

*The G20 sits outside a restaurant while McCluster waits for a DoorDash order. He checks the order, the time, and his remaining gas. The food is somebody else's dinner. The delivery is part of his next stretch of road.*

**MCCLUSTER — PHONE:** The food's not ready yet.

**MAJID:** You eating?

**MCCLUSTER:** Not this food. This food has an address.

**MAJID:** You know what I meant.

**MCCLUSTER:** I'll get something when I get gas.

**MAJID:** You said that at the last stop.

**MCCLUSTER:** At the last stop I was going to get gas when I got something. Different plan.

*His order is called. He collects it, grateful to have something to do before Majid can answer that.*

*A completed delivery. A fuel stop. A little more road. These are ordinary tasks seen long enough to feel their repetition. There is no instant windfall. The delivery money and the fuel both count; one does not make the other free.*

*Later, parked. McCluster has the phone propped where Majid can see him. His jacket is folded under his head. Outside, someone shuts a car door, and both men go quiet until the sound passes.*

**MAJID:** What was that?

**MCCLUSTER:** Somebody getting into their car.

**MAJID:** All right.

**MCCLUSTER:** You asked that like you're out here with me.

**MAJID:** You're the one who called me.

**MCCLUSTER:** I'm glad you answered.

*Majid lets that sit without turning it into a speech.*

**MAJID:** How's the van doing?

**MCCLUSTER:** Good. That's the crazy part. I thought I'd be calling you about the van. The van's doing fine.

**MAJID:** Then what's wrong?

**MCCLUSTER:** Nothing's wrong. It's just a lot of road when you're the only one in it.

**MAJID:** You've been awake a while.

**MCCLUSTER:** I know.

**MAJID:** You're parked now. Stay there for a little bit.

**MCCLUSTER:** I'm going to sleep. I just didn't want to hang up yet.

**MAJID:** Then don't.

*McCluster lies back. Their conversation becomes less organized. Majid starts telling him something from his own day, loses the thread, starts again. Nothing in the story advances a business plan. That is why McCluster can finally stop performing one.*

*Morning. The call has ended. McCluster wakes in the van and reaches for the phone before he reaches for his shoes.*

**MCCLUSTER — TEXT:** Still here. Still got the hotel.

**MAJID — TEXT:** Go eat breakfast.

### Georgia, then north again

*Georgia is a stop in the van's life, not its sale destination. Later, the G20 goes north to Connecticut. Exact dates and the length of the gaps remain open.*

*In Connecticut, McCluster takes the belongings out of the van for listing photographs. Without the clothes and food, the sleeping space looks less like his room and more like the thing a buyer has been looking for. He takes the photograph, then puts his life back inside.*

*A call comes in. This is the prospective Boston buyer who will later buy the green Econoline, not the eventual Long Island G20 buyer. This short exchange is invented to stage the remembered lead connection.*

**PROSPECTIVE BOSTON BUYER:** Still have the G20?

**MCCLUSTER:** Right now, yes.

**PROSPECTIVE BOSTON BUYER:** I'm looking for something like that. I've got a few things I want to ask you.

*McCluster sits on the sleeper and listens. The questions keep coming. He answers them, sends what he can, and learns what the caller wants. A conversation can continue even when a sale does not happen.*

*Later, another buyer is ready to meet on Long Island. McCluster packs his things into bags. The same space becomes a vehicle for sale again.*

**MAJID — PHONE:** How are you getting back?

**MCCLUSTER:** Train.

**MAJID:** You checked that before you agreed to go?

**MCCLUSTER:** I know there's a train.

**MAJID:** That's a strong beginning.

*McCluster looks up the return journey. This small check is invented staging, not evidence of a particular station or ticket.*

**MCCLUSTER:** Yes. Train back to Connecticut.

**MAJID:** And all that stuff?

**MCCLUSTER:** Coming with me.

**MAJID:** You own less stuff when it has to go on a train.

*McCluster looks at the bags and begins deciding what he can actually carry.*

### Long Island

*The G20 sale. Long Island is the working location after the owner's spoken correction. No town, train line, or precise date is assigned.*

*The buyer inspects the sleeping area while McCluster waits beside the open door.*

**G20 BUYER:** You've used the back yourself?

**MCCLUSTER:** Yeah. That's not a demonstration bed. I've been in there.

**G20 BUYER:** Where did you bring it from?

**MCCLUSTER:** Michigan. Then Georgia. Then Connecticut. Now here.

**G20 BUYER:** Why all that way?

**MCCLUSTER:** It was where I needed to go.

*The buyer settles into the driver's seat. McCluster steps away to give the van room to belong to somebody else.*

*The sale closes at approximately the remembered $2,500. The exact exchange and buyer's dialogue are dramatized; no new document or transaction detail is established.*

*Later, on the train to Connecticut. His bags are wedged around his feet. The ground is moving beneath him and he is not responsible for keeping a vehicle on the road. For the first time in a while, he can look out the window as long as he wants.*

**MAJID — PHONE:** You on the train?

**MCCLUSTER:** Yeah.

**MAJID:** How does it feel?

**MCCLUSTER:** Weird. I sold my bedroom and bought a train ride.

**MAJID:** You made your sale.

**MCCLUSTER:** I know. It got me all that way, too. That's the part I keep thinking about.

**MAJID:** It did what you needed.

**MCCLUSTER:** Yeah. It did.

*He watches the passing lights. The buyer has a van. McCluster has some money and the bags at his feet. There is satisfaction in it, and a small vacancy he did not price.*

*His phone lights up with another question from the Boston lead.*

**MAJID:** What's next?

**MCCLUSTER:** That guy who wanted the G20. He's still looking.

**MAJID:** You just sold it.

**MCCLUSTER:** He doesn't need my G20. He needs a van.

*McCluster starts looking.*


## III. The green Econoline

*Near Boston, in the same approximate period. The van is green and remembered as an E-150/Econoline; its year is unresolved. It cost about $1,500 and sold for about $2,600 or $2,700 within roughly a month or less. The buyer is anonymized. The precise vehicle-transport logistics are not reconstructed.*

### 1. The voice on the phone

*Near Boston. Day. McCluster leans against the green van, phone at his ear. The caller has become familiar enough that McCluster no longer needs to look at the screen.*

**BOSTON BUYER:** I still think you should have held that G20 for me.

**MCCLUSTER:** You called about a van. Somebody else bought a van. Those are different stages of the process.

**BOSTON BUYER:** I was serious.

**MCCLUSTER:** You've been serious on the phone for several days. Come look at this one.

*The buyer laughs. He has stories, opinions, another question about the Ford, then another story. McCluster lets him talk while inspecting a small imperfection in the paint.*

*The conversation turns ugly without the buyer changing his friendly tone. He boasts about beating a Black man. His account includes the N-word; the word is not repeated in this draft. He assumes McCluster will share his attitude.*

*McCluster stops rubbing the paint.*

**MCCLUSTER:** That's what you're telling a guy you're buying a van from?

**BOSTON BUYER:** I'm just talking. You know how people are.

**MCCLUSTER:** You seem pretty sure you know how I am.

**BOSTON BUYER:** I can hear where you're from.

*McCluster looks down at himself, then at the green Ford. The ridiculousness of the assumption gets a laugh out of him. Instead of correcting the man, he plays along with the racist joking, borrowing the caller's tone and letting him believe they agree. No additional slur is written into the exchange.*

**MCCLUSTER:** Oh, yeah. You got me all figured out.

**BOSTON BUYER:** So when can I see it?

**MCCLUSTER:** We could have scheduled that four stories ago.

*They arrange the meeting. After the call, McCluster keeps the phone in his hand a moment. His smile has gone. He has chosen to let the man's assumption survive until they meet; now he has to decide whether that was funny or a complication.*

### 2. Same guy

*The meeting. The green Econoline is unmistakable. The buyer approaches it, looks at McCluster, then looks past him.*

**BOSTON BUYER:** I'm looking for McCluster.

**MCCLUSTER:** You found him.

**BOSTON BUYER:** No. The guy I've been talking to.

**MCCLUSTER:** About the G20 you didn't buy and this van you might buy.

*The voice lands. The buyer looks back toward the road as if another explanation might be arriving.*

**BOSTON BUYER:** You're not the same guy.

**MCCLUSTER:** Yes, I am. Is that going to be a problem for us doing business?

*The buyer opens his mouth. Nothing useful comes out.*

**MCCLUSTER:** That's a real question. We can settle it before we spend another hour talking about a van.

**BOSTON BUYER:** No. No problem. I thought—

**MCCLUSTER:** I know what you thought.

**BOSTON BUYER:** Your voice. You sounded—

**MCCLUSTER:** Exactly like this?

*The buyer gives a short, embarrassed laugh. McCluster does not join it.*

**BOSTON BUYER:** I owe you an apology.

**MCCLUSTER:** You do.

**BOSTON BUYER:** I'm sorry. For what I said. All that stuff I was saying.

**MCCLUSTER:** I heard you. You were comfortable saying it.

*That takes longer to answer.*

**BOSTON BUYER:** I was wrong.

**MCCLUSTER:** All right. Do you still want to look at the Ford?

*He does. The inspection gives both men something concrete to discuss. The buyer asks ordinary questions in a newly careful voice. McCluster answers them. When the buyer apologizes again, McCluster points him back toward something he wanted to inspect.*

**BOSTON BUYER:** This is exactly the sort of van I was looking for. I really do think I'm getting a good deal.

**MCCLUSTER:** That's what we've been talking about. You needed a van. I found you a van.

*They finish the agreed sale. The recollection leaves the price at approximately $2,600 or $2,700; the scene does not select an exact total.*

### 3. An unusual apology

*Later, during the remaining errands, the buyer suggests a stop at a Massachusetts dispensary. He offers to buy McCluster a hundred-dollar gift.*

**MCCLUSTER:** You don't have to buy me something.

**BOSTON BUYER:** I want to. I've been thinking about that phone call the whole time.

**MCCLUSTER:** There were several. You have a selection.

**BOSTON BUYER:** I know. That's what I'm saying.

*Inside, the buyer suddenly looks less certain about his idea.*

**BOSTON BUYER:** Wait. Am I doing it again?

**MCCLUSTER:** Doing what?

**BOSTON BUYER:** Making an assumption. Buying you weed. Is that another stereotype?

*McCluster looks at him, then at the counter.*

**MCCLUSTER:** You are, but fuck it. I'll take the weed.

*McCluster laughs this time. The buyer laughs with relief, then catches himself, uncertain how much relief he has earned.*

**BOSTON BUYER:** I really am sorry.

**MCCLUSTER:** I believe you're sorry right now. You can let me pick something without saying it again.

*Outside, McCluster checks his phone. The buyer attends to an ordinary errand a short distance away. McCluster shares his location with his sister.*

**MCCLUSTER — TEXT:** That's where I am. The van guy. The one I told you about.

**SISTER — TEXT:** The guy from the phone?

**MCCLUSTER — TEXT:** Yes. He's been apologizing since he saw me.

**SISTER — TEXT:** Are you okay?

**MCCLUSTER — TEXT:** I'm okay. A little nervous. Just keep the location.

**SISTER — TEXT:** You can leave. You sold the van.

*McCluster reads that twice. He glances toward the buyer, who is waiting without beckoning him.*

**MCCLUSTER — TEXT:** I know. Finishing up helping him get back. I'll check in.

*He pockets the phone, then takes it out again and turns the sound up.*

### 4. The shed

*At the buyer's house. The arrangements for getting there are complete. McCluster has a chance to leave. Then the buyer remembers something he offered to show him.*

**BOSTON BUYER:** You want to see the cigarette thing before you go?

**MCCLUSTER:** What cigarette thing?

**BOSTON BUYER:** How I make them. I told you about it.

**MCCLUSTER:** You told me a lot of things.

*The buyer winces.*

**BOSTON BUYER:** It's out in the shed.

*McCluster looks at the shed.*

**MCCLUSTER:** Of course it is.

**BOSTON BUYER:** What?

**MCCLUSTER:** Nothing. Give me a second.

**MCCLUSTER — TEXT:** He wants to show me something in his shed.

**SISTER — TEXT:** McCluster.

*He can hear her voice in that single word. He looks at the buyer again, then chooses to follow, phone in hand.*

*The shed door remains open. Inside is an ordinary work surface. The buyer clears a place, embarrassed by the clutter. McCluster stands near the entrance, letting his eyes adjust.*

**BOSTON BUYER:** You can come closer.

**MCCLUSTER:** I can see from here.

*The buyer begins his demonstration. We see his practiced hands and the finished cigarettes, not a sequence of instructions. He works with the absorption of someone showing off a small skill he has spent years getting right.*

*McCluster watches. Nothing sinister happens. His shoulders gradually lower.*

**MCCLUSTER:** Okay. You're actually good at that.

**BOSTON BUYER:** Been doing it a long time.

**MCCLUSTER:** I can tell. That's a completely reasonable thing to have in a shed.

*The buyer looks up. The joke takes a second to reach him.*

**BOSTON BUYER:** You were worried?

**MCCLUSTER:** Think about the information you've given me today.

*The buyer stops working. His embarrassment has become quieter.*

**BOSTON BUYER:** I'm sorry I made you feel like that.

**MCCLUSTER:** Thank you.

*McCluster lets the answer stand. He does not supply reassurance to make the silence easier for the other man.*

*Then his phone sounds.*

**SISTER — TEXT:** Answer me.

**MCCLUSTER — TEXT:** I'm good. He actually makes cigarettes.

**SISTER — TEXT:** That was never the part I was asking about.

*McCluster laughs, then calls her.*

### 5. Afterward

*McCluster has said goodbye. The buyer and his green van are behind him. McCluster has his own space again, with his sister on the phone.*

**SISTER:** You're away from the house?

**MCCLUSTER:** Yeah.

**SISTER:** Good. Start with that next time.

**MCCLUSTER:** He was very apologetic. I think seeing me really did something to him.

**SISTER:** Maybe.

**MCCLUSTER:** Maybe. I'm not saying I know what's in his heart. He seemed different by the time I left.

**SISTER:** I'm glad you got to leave and think about it.

*McCluster looks at the gift, then shakes his head at the length of the day.*

**MCCLUSTER:** This man called me about a G20.

**SISTER:** And you still managed to spend all day with him.

**MCCLUSTER:** I am never answering another question about that G20.

*He stays on the line with her.*
