## Controlling story and character continuity

In the current dramatic adaptation, McCluster begins in Bridgeport working and living from vehicles, with a legitimate service business and ambitions beyond what its current income can support. Test Drive introduces the woman who becomes the continuing romantic lead. Its attraction, deal assessment, driving lesson, and criminal implications belong together.

McCluster knowingly joins Bosco's criminal-capital partnership to expand legitimate dealer, fleet, and service operations. He is attracted to the opportunity and accepts responsibility for entering it. The later discoveries concern the operation's scale, coercion, compounds, treatment of people, and control. They are not his first discovery that criminal activity exists.

Business growth brings a new location, the earlier supervised Bridgeport–Nantucket charter with Bosco and Madé, and expansion into Atlanta and Acworth. That early charter is distinct from both the later Jet obligation and Alana's fatal flight. Locations, passengers, and motives must remain consistent even when images from the trips recur in later videos.

Rico's illegal resale of a financed salvage car creates the Repo Guy conflict. Majid subsequently advises McCluster to build enough independent money to leave his gangster partners. The alliance develops because its members have different reasons to reject Bosco's control. Darrel earns access through dispatch and receiving, betrays that alliance, and is killed by Madé's intervention. The current screenplay supplies the particular confrontation and aftermath around that approved result.

Alana personifies Atlanta's promise: love, glamour, money, expansion, and escape become entangled with a trap. She is the same person throughout, with enough ordinary affection, humor, work, and disappointment for the audience to care about her loss. Her identity is confirmed owner canon, not a provisional casting suggestion.

In the ending, **Prime warns and McCluster independently chooses and causes the aircraft outcome**. Alana dies. The older board-ordered execution premise is superseded. Salvage then closes the story through the drive-away, memories, blame, depletion, and the attempt to keep moving. Any appearance of Alana after her death must be an identifiable memory or subjective image, not a new physical encounter.

## Preserve each song's meanings

The songs may move between speakers, performance, private feeling, and contradictory perspectives. Their surrounding scenes need not prove every boast correct. Humor and menace can coexist with real competence and affection.

- **Test Drive:** preserve the driving, deal-reading, seduction, and illicit meanings. A purely ordinary used-car romance omits part of the owner's treatment.
- **Mr. Lock Changer:** McCluster conducts legitimate mobile automotive service. His cousin has already received a tablet from him and uses it to steal cars. The same first-person lyrics can belong to either man. Payments, tools, confidence, police nerves, imitation, and the fob-breaker/lock-changer distinction carry the comedy and double meaning. The cousin is not rewritten into an apprentice before the theft happens.
- **Whip Equipped:** growth, the new location, customer approval, business travel, parental expectations, and suspicion about the surrounding circle motivate the title chapter. The separate survival/faith material in the older treatment is not silently combined with the opening-location sheet. The recording-to-lyric match still needs resolution.
- **Lana:** retain Alana/Atlanta, the high-rise, glamour, money, scammers, Ferrari track, Grandma in Atlanta, Savannah, Acworth growth, rentals, and the dangerous mindset beneath apparent success.
- **Repo Guy:** the recovered vehicle is the same financed salvage car Rico sold. Armed resistance and the short disarming confrontation end with **no shots fired**. Preserve the street buyer's loss as part of the situation.
- **Hundun:** earning, accumulation, family, tribe, roadside support, and a gradually built foundation remain central. The compound plot belongs in surrounding story material rather than replacing the song with a laboratory reveal.
- **All in the Details:** service advertising and detailing coexist with shady vehicles, snakes, rats, toxic waste, the masked client, and the Cadillac/body imagery. Preserve the tension between legitimate car flipping and criminal associations.
- **Towyard Killer:** the conflict concerns an impoundment and inflated fees challenged through records and documents. The title does not make its music video a tow-yard homicide.
- **Stand On Business:** practical competence, written plans, terms, commitments, and a recap using earlier video imagery hold the journey together.
- **Dealer Plates:** freedom from the dealer mafia and legitimate/criminal duality coexist. The settlement changes business control; it does not invent a first-ever license after the earlier Atlanta paperwork.
- **Tears Won't Fall:** the sung narrator remains alone in his car. The aircraft images show the consequence of his choice while numbness, doubt, screened calls, and self-justification continue in his perspective.
- **Salvage Title:** keep the car/relationship rebuilding metaphor, mutual depletion, blame, cutting losses, and next-flip language. Heard after the death, these express his perspective; they do not create a second living breakup.

The fictional compound thread includes Lazarus, black liquid, and pills, and exposes coercion through access and dependence. These story constructs do not establish real chemistry or medical facts. Newly detailed effects must remain consistent with the fictional rules actually established.
