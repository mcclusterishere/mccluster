# Equity Uprise Competency Architecture

> Status: CANONICAL COMPETENCY ARCHITECTURE v0.1

## Purpose

The Development Passport uses a competency graph rather than a course checklist.

A participant advances because evidence shows increasingly independent capability, not because they accumulated hours or badges.

## Canonical maturity ladder

1. Aware — understands what the competency is and why it matters.
2. Trained — completed relevant structured learning.
3. Practicing — used the skill in a controlled or supervised exercise.
4. Demonstrated — produced observable work using the competency.
5. Verified — qualified review confirms independent performance meets the standard.
6. Applied — used the competency successfully in an authentic setting.
7. Mentor — consistently performs it and successfully guides or reviews others.

Maturity and authorization are independent. Verified or Mentor status never automatically grants protected-system access.

## Credentials are evidence, not mastery

A federal or external credential may support one or more competencies.

The issuer's actual award type must be preserved accurately: certification, certificate of completion, training record, CE record, apprenticeship credential, or another form may not be relabeled for marketing convenience.

Training normally contributes toward Trained.

Demonstrated and Verified require performance evidence.

Applied requires authentic use.

Mentor requires repeated applied evidence and successful guidance/review of another person.

## Projects

Projects bundle competencies.

A single authentic project can support several competency assessments while each competency retains its own rubric.

Progressive project authority:

assist → contribute → own a deliverable → own a project → lead a team → mentor

## Prerequisites

Competency prerequisites allow the system to reveal complexity progressively.

Examples:

- basic research literacy before advanced research design;
- ethics/consent before human-participant research;
- data/provenance before spatial intelligence;
- security fundamentals before advanced operational tool use;
- professional practice before consequential public-program delivery.

Prerequisites support readiness. They do not replace authorization.
