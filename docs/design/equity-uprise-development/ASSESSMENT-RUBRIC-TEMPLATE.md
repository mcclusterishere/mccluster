# Equity Uprise Canonical Assessment Rubric Template

> Status: CANONICAL RUBRIC TEMPLATE v1.0

## 1. Universal assessment dimensions

Every competency rubric is interpreted through six common dimensions.

### Knowledge & Judgment
Does the participant understand what they are doing, why, the applicable limits, and relevant risks?

### Execution
Can the participant actually perform the competency correctly?

### Evidence / Quality
Is the resulting work accurate, complete, defensible, and fit for purpose?

### Independence
How much substantive direction or intervention was required?

### Professional Practice
Were communication, ethics, documentation, deadlines, privacy, safety, and responsibilities handled appropriately?

### Reflection & Transfer
Can the participant explain what happened, identify improvements, and transfer the competency into a changed context?

Competency-specific rubrics normally define 4–6 observable criteria that instantiate these dimensions.

## 2. Criterion performance scale

### 0 — Not Yet Demonstrated
Evidence is absent, materially incorrect, unsafe, or insufficient to determine capability.

### 1 — Developing
Partial understanding/performance; substantial correction or guidance is required.

### 2 — Meets Standard With Support
Appropriate performance is possible with reasonable guidance, templates, prompts, or intervention.

### 3 — Meets Standard Independently
Performs appropriately and reliably without substantive intervention.

This is the normal performance threshold for Verified.

### 4 — Advanced / Can Guide Others
Performs independently across complexity, anticipates problems, explains judgment, and can effectively guide/review others.

A single 4 does not automatically create Mentor status.

## 3. Critical criteria

A criterion can be marked critical.

Critical requirements include, where applicable:

- safety;
- consent;
- privacy;
- research ethics;
- cybersecurity/access control;
- financial handling;
- rights/licensing;
- emergency-role discipline;
- preservation of decision-maker authority.

Critical failures cannot be offset by high performance elsewhere.

Default rule for Verified:
all critical criteria must score at least 3.

## 4. Maturity vs rubric performance

The 0–4 criterion score describes performance quality.

Passport maturity describes evidence-backed developmental state.

They are related but not identical.

A participant can perform at Level 3 in a simulation and still not be Applied because Applied requires authentic context.

## 5. Default Verified rule

Unless a competency overrides it:

- substantive qualifying evidence exists;
- required prerequisites are satisfied;
- all critical criteria >= 3;
- no criterion = 0;
- majority of applicable criteria >= 3;
- overall outcome = verified_independent or advanced;
- assistance = independent or leading;
- authorized reviewer;
- complete provenance.

## 6. Default Applied rule

- competency already meets Verified standard;
- qualifying performance occurs in supervised-authentic or authentic-independent context as appropriate;
- performance still meets the competency standard.

## 7. Default Mentor rule

- repeated Applied evidence;
- Advanced-quality performance;
- successful mentoring/reviewer evidence;
- leadership/mentoring context;
- authorized assessment.

## 8. Assessment outcomes

### needs_development
Evidence does not yet establish the required capability.

### demonstrated_with_support
Useful capability is present but substantive support is still required.

### verified_independent
Independent performance meets the standard.

### advanced
Independent performance exceeds normal standard and demonstrates advanced judgment.

## 9. Assistance

Record separately:

- directed;
- supported;
- independent;
- leading.

## 10. Context

Record separately:

- instructional;
- simulation;
- supervised_authentic;
- authentic_independent;
- leadership_mentoring.

## 11. AI assistance

Record separately:

- none;
- assistive;
- collaborative;
- substantial_generation;
- automated_workflow.

AI use may trigger an Evidence Defense where human judgment is not directly observable.

## 12. Rubric metadata

Every canonical rubric should define:

- rubric_id;
- competency_id;
- version;
- effective date;
- competency definition;
- prerequisites;
- accepted evidence;
- required evidence;
- maturity targets;
- criteria;
- critical criteria;
- minimum independence;
- minimum context;
- eligible reviewer roles;
- evidence-defense rule;
- recency/refresh policy;
- integrity requirements;
- reassessment policy.

## 13. Criterion schema

Each criterion should include:

- criterion_id;
- name;
- description;
- critical boolean;
- observable indicators;
- the shared 0–4 scale;
- optional competency-specific anchors where the shared scale is insufficient.

## 14. Feedback

Every assessment should produce:

- what the participant demonstrated;
- what needs development;
- the next action.

Assessment should guide development rather than merely label failure.

## 15. Multiple competencies from one artifact

One artifact may support several competency assessments.

Each competency receives a separate rubric judgment.

Formal assessment should prioritize a manageable number of primary competencies so reviewers are not forced to score everything a project could theoretically evidence.
