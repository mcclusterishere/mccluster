# Equity Uprise Evidence & Assessment Architecture

> Status: CANONICAL EVIDENCE / ASSESSMENT AUTHORITY v0.1

## 1. Governing principle

Equity Uprise does not award competency because somebody clicked Complete.

Competency is supported by evidence.

The Passport derives maturity from learning, performance, artifacts, context, independence, qualified review, provenance, and recency.

## 2. Seven competency states

Aware
→ Trained
→ Practicing
→ Demonstrated
→ Verified
→ Applied
→ Mentor

### Aware
Understands the basic concept and why it matters.

Typical evidence:
- orientation;
- diagnostic;
- introductory activity.

### Trained
Completed relevant structured learning.

Typical evidence:
- approved federal/external course;
- Equity Uprise learning module;
- structured instruction record.

Training alone normally cannot establish Verified status.

### Practicing
Used the skill in a controlled or supervised exercise.

### Demonstrated
Produced observable work or performance that substantively uses the competency.

### Verified
An authorized reviewer confirms that independent performance meets the canonical standard.

### Applied
Used Verified-quality capability in an authentic setting with real accountability or consequence.

### Mentor
Shows repeated Applied performance and can successfully guide or review others.

## 3. Five evidence classes

### Training evidence
Shows structured learning.

Examples:
- federal completion record;
- course certificate;
- Equity Uprise training record.

Normally supports Aware → Trained.

### Artifact evidence
Something the participant created.

Examples:
- research memo;
- source matrix;
- map;
- video;
- interview protocol;
- website;
- budget;
- project plan;
- presentation;
- data analysis.

Normally supports Practicing → Demonstrated.

### Performance evidence
Observed behavior.

Examples:
- presentation;
- simulation;
- stakeholder interview;
- facilitated meeting;
- briefing;
- live tool operation;
- evidence defense.

Supports Practicing, Demonstrated, or Verified depending on quality, independence, context, and reviewer authority.

### Applied evidence
Use in an authentic environment.

Examples:
- real community service;
- real Equity Uprise project;
- client work;
- publication;
- internship/apprenticeship activity;
- authentic organization engagement;
- operational decision support.

Required for Applied status.

### Reviewer evidence
Human assessment tied to a canonical rubric.

Required for Verified and normally required again for Mentor.

## 4. Evidence quality dimensions

Every important evidence item may be evaluated for:

- Authenticity — did the participant actually do the work?
- Relevance — does the evidence genuinely bear on the claimed competency?
- Quality — does the work meet the applicable standard?
- Independence — how much substantive assistance was required?
- Context — instructional, simulated, supervised authentic, authentic independent, or leadership/mentoring?
- Recency — is the evidence still representative where currency matters?

## 5. Assistance levels

Directed
- explicit step-by-step guidance.

Supported
- periodic substantive help.

Independent
- no substantive intervention required.

Leading
- directs others or makes higher-level decisions.

High-quality work created with Supported assistance is not automatically evidence of independent Verified capability.

## 6. Context levels

Instructional
- controlled learning task.

Simulation
- realistic but artificial scenario.

Supervised authentic
- real work under active oversight.

Authentic independent
- real work under normal professional accountability.

Leadership / mentoring
- participant is responsible for guiding/reviewing others.

Applied status requires authentic context.

## 7. AI assistance

AI use is recorded rather than prohibited.

Canonical disclosure values:

- none;
- assistive;
- collaborative;
- substantial_generation;
- automated_workflow.

AI use does not automatically invalidate evidence.

The reviewer must still establish the human competency being claimed.

Where AI materially creates the output, an evidence defense, live performance, or other direct demonstration may be required.

## 8. Evidence defense

A reviewer may request an Evidence Defense when the artifact alone does not establish participant understanding.

Typical prompts:

1. Explain what you did.
2. Explain important decisions.
3. Identify limitations.
4. Respond to a challenge, error, or changed scenario.
5. Explain what you would improve.

Typical duration may range from approximately 5–20 minutes depending on competency and risk.

## 9. Verified status

Default Verified policy:

- at least one substantive qualifying artifact or performance;
- prerequisite training where explicitly required;
- all critical rubric criteria passed;
- no rubric criterion at Not Yet Demonstrated;
- majority of applicable criteria at Meets Standard Independently or Advanced;
- overall reviewer determination of independent standard;
- authorized reviewer;
- complete provenance;
- assistance compatible with independent performance.

Verified is not calculated solely by averaging points.

Critical safety, ethics, privacy, security, consent, rights, or authority requirements cannot be averaged away.

## 10. Applied status

Applied requires Verified-quality performance in an authentic environment.

A sandbox, classroom-only exercise, or fictional simulation does not become Applied simply because the artifact quality is high.

## 11. Mentor status

Mentor is not merely a high rubric score.

Default Mentor policy requires:

- repeated Applied evidence;
- meaningful evidence across at least two contexts where appropriate;
- Advanced-quality judgment;
- successful guidance/review of another participant;
- positive assessment of the mentoring activity;
- no unresolved material integrity issue.

## 12. Credentials vs competencies

Credential state and competency state remain separate.

Example:

FEMA IS-100.C
- credential/training state: completed

Emergency & Incident Coordination
- competency state: practicing

A credential may expire or require refresh while underlying demonstrated capability remains in the Passport.

Operational assignment can still require current training even when the competency remains Verified.

## 13. Reviewer classes

### Level 1 — Automated verifier
May verify:
- artifact existence;
- metadata;
- expected document/certificate form;
- required fields;
- deterministic validation.

May not independently award human-performance Verified status.

### Level 2 — Peer reviewer
May provide developmental feedback and, where policy allows, assess Practicing/Demonstrated evidence.

### Level 3 — Equity Uprise reviewer
Authorized to award Verified status for assigned competency domains.

### Level 4 — Domain reviewer
Faculty, professional, partner, subject expert, or equivalent authorized for advanced/domain-specific assessment.

### Level 5 — Program authority
May approve rubric/reviewer policy, exceptions, disputed cases, and formal standard changes.

Reviewer authority is competency-specific, not a global reviewer=true flag.

## 14. Reassessment and appeal

A weak submission should generally return:

- what was demonstrated;
- what needs development;
- the next action;
- what must be resubmitted.

Canonical assessment outcomes:

- needs_development;
- demonstrated_with_support;
- verified_independent;
- advanced.

Participants may request reassessment according to program policy.

Contested assessments may receive a second qualified review.

## 15. Integrity / provenance

Evidence records should preserve:

- participant identity;
- evidence ID;
- competency IDs supported;
- evidence class;
- title and description;
- artifact location/reference;
- project ID;
- external issuer where applicable;
- completion/submission date;
- authenticity status;
- AI assistance declaration;
- assistance level;
- context;
- reviewer identity;
- rubric version;
- assessment result;
- visibility;
- expiration/renewal metadata where applicable;
- provenance and version/hash data.

A verified artifact may not be silently overwritten.

Materially revised work receives a new version while the evidence supporting the original verification remains traceable.

## 16. Rubric versions

Assessment standards are versioned.

A historical verification records the exact rubric version used.

If a later standard changes materially, the Passport may indicate:
- verified under prior standard;
- refresh recommended or required.

Past decisions do not silently mutate.

## 17. Cohort readiness

Cohorts may express transparent competency thresholds.

Example:

- CORE-01 >= Demonstrated
- CORE-07 >= Verified
- SHR-01 >= Practicing
- SHR-03 >= Trained
- OPS-01 >= Practicing

The Passport can then show what is satisfied, what remains, and the next recommended action.

Readiness does not guarantee admission when legitimate capacity or selection constraints exist.

## 18. Tool access

Competency thresholds may be one input into tool access, but competence never substitutes for authorization.

Consequential access may require:

identity/role
+ competency threshold
+ project assignment
+ explicit approval.

## 19. Evidence object principle

Conceptually, competency state is a function of:

training
+ performance
+ artifact
+ context
+ independence
+ qualified review
+ provenance
+ recency

not hours watched or quiz completion alone.


## Federal training evidence resolution

Federal courses/programs used as external evidence resolve through `FEDERAL-TRAINING-CATALOG.json` and `FEDERAL-TRAINING-BINDINGS.json`. Issuer completion/credential state remains separate from competency state; federal training can support a claim, but the applicable rubric, artifact quality, reviewer judgment and evidence-defense rules still control competency mastery.

## 20. Lab Runtime Step 6 implementation

The executable Equity Uprise lab runtime implements this authority through a bounded Level 1 automated assessment layer.

Canonical implementation:
- `../equity-uprise-building/production/lab-runtime/equity-uprise-assessment-runtime.mjs`
- `../equity-uprise-building/production/lab-runtime/assessment-policy-v1.json`
- `../equity-uprise-building/production/lab-runtime/verify_assessment_runtime_v1.mjs`

The runtime records:
- action sequence and outcomes;
- timing, including diagnosis/restoration milestones;
- hints and assistance level;
- safety violations;
- produced evidence references and hashes;
- AI-assistance declaration;
- competency/rubric provenance.

Machine output is intentionally bounded. It may emit `practicing_evidence` or `demonstrated_evidence_candidate` signals, but it does not assign rubric criterion scores or award `Verified`, `Applied`, or `Mentor`.

Critical safety/privacy/ethics/consent/security/authority violations are explicit gates and cannot be averaged away by otherwise successful actions.

Simulation evidence remains simulation evidence. `Applied` still requires authentic context, and `Verified` still requires an authorized qualified reviewer under the governing policy above.
