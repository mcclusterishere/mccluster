## 2. Finish-pass objective

The next exterior pass must make the building read as a finished institutional headquarters from normal human-eye street perspectives.

The facade must stop reading as:
- flat procedural panels;
- translucent colored boxes;
- thin caps;
- generic mullion grids;
- placeholder entry geometry;
- pixel-like architectural signage;
- roof objects merely placed behind a facade band.

The facade should instead read as a coherent installed assembly with believable:
- depth;
- joints;
- transitions;
- material response;
- shadow lines;
- service logic;
- entrance hierarchy;
- crown termination;
- ground contact.

## 3. Non-negotiable preservation rules

The finish pass may add geometric depth and detail but may not casually change:
- the 72 ft × 72 ft shell;
- floor elevations;
- 18 ft structural rhythm;
- 6 ft facade module;
- Stair A or Stair B;
- passenger elevator;
- freight/service elevator;
- MEP/riser reservation;
- approved interior program geometry;
- Floor 1 entrance/vestibule alignment;
- Floor 4 acoustic exception;
- Level 7 open-air condition.

If a finish detail conflicts with any of these, the detail moves — not the building chassis.

## 4. Window / curtain-wall assembly finish

Every vision or privacy-glass module must read as an installed facade assembly rather than a flat glass plane.

Later geometry must include, where applicable:
- glazing recessed behind the primary facade face;
- jamb depth;
- head depth;
- sill depth;
- perimeter shadow reveal;
- stronger primary mullion hierarchy;
- thinner secondary mullion hierarchy;
- slab-edge / spandrel transition depth;
- controlled gasket/shadow-joint implication;
- resolved glass-to-opaque-panel transition;
- intentional corner returns.

The finish pass should preserve the 6 ft module while making each bay materially and geometrically legible.

## 5. Corner finish

All four exterior corners require an intentional assembly.

A corner may resolve as:
- glass-to-glass;
- glass-to-metal corner pier;
- opaque mineral/metal corner pier;
- service/core corner condition.

No elevation may simply terminate its panels at the corner and rely on overlap.

South corners should remain visually lighter where compatible with the interior. North corners may remain heavier because of core/service adjacency.

## 6. Floor 1 base / plinth finish

The base should visually carry the building.

Required finish direction:
- deeper mineral/plinth geometry;
- real panel/reveal rhythm;
- visible transition from plinth to upper facade;
- stronger shadow line at the base;
- intentional threshold into the entrance zone;
- no appearance of upper curtain wall simply touching the ground plane.

The base should remain restrained and civic, not monumental for its own sake.

## 7. South entrance finish

The principal entrance is a mandatory architectural focal point.

Later finish geometry must include:
- deeper entry portal;
- actual paired door leaves;
- door frames;
- transom / sidelight hierarchy;
- handle/pull-bar geometry;
- vestibule depth;
- threshold geometry;
- canopy fascia;
- canopy soffit;
- recessed canopy lighting;
- credible canopy support/cantilever expression;
- real mounting planes for branding;
- directory / wayfinding adjacency;
- ground/apron transition.

The entrance must no longer read as a framed opening plus a slab.

## 8. Architectural branding finish

The exact approved Equity Uprise logo from `assets/img/equity-uprise-logo.webp` and the Equity Uprise wordmark must replace the procedural/block entrance identity. Literal `E=U` text is not the approved logo.

The finished sign system should use:
- dimensional letter/mark depth;
- clean geometry;
- consistent letter spacing;
- real backing or mounting plane;
- restrained halo/backlighting where used;
- coordinated day/night legibility.

Primary identity remains at the south entrance.

A single restrained crown use of the same approved Equity Uprise logo remains allowed. It must not become billboard-scale.

## 9. Canopy finish

The canopy should become a real architectural element.

Required finish:
- fascia thickness;
- soffit plane;
- edge return;
- support/cantilever logic;
- recessed light fixtures;
- drainage concept expressed subtly enough to avoid fake detailing;
- material transition into entry portal.

The canopy may project beyond the south control plane as already authorized.

## 10. Crown / roof-edge finish

The current crown must be upgraded from a thin termination into a resolved top-of-building composition.

Later geometry should coordinate:
- Floor 6 crown band depth;
- parapet caps;
- roof-edge shadow line;
- roof-service screens;
- stair-headhouse integration;
- elevator-overrun/service enclosure integration;
- north service-screen rhythm;
- crown signage backing where used;
- roof lighting support.

Level 7 must remain visibly open-air. The finish pass may not create a fake enclosed seventh floor.

## 11. North/service facade finish

The north elevation may remain the most opaque face, but it must look intentionally designed.

Later finish work should refine:
- stair-light slots;
- support clerestories;
- louver depth;
- panel joint hierarchy;
- service-screen continuity;
- opaque-panel transitions;
- corner/core articulation.

No decorative louver is allowed without a real service/MEP purpose behind it.

## 12. Material realism

The finish pass must move beyond flat face-color behavior.

Required material families remain:
- `FAC-GLASS-01` — clear vision glass;
- `FAC-GLASS-02` — privacy/frit glass;
- `FAC-METAL-01` — dark mullions/frames/fins;
- `FAC-OPAQUE-01` — spandrel/service/crown panel;
- `FAC-MINERAL-01` — grounded base/plinth;
- `FAC-WARM-01` — limited entrance accent.

Finish direction:
- glass should reflect environment while remaining transparent enough to reveal inhabited interiors;
- privacy glass should remain in the same visual family;
- metal should catch highlights without becoming chrome;
- mineral/base surfaces should feel heavier and rougher than metal;
- warm accent material should stay concentrated at the entrance.

Exact manufacturer/product selection remains unresolved.

## 13. Exterior lighting finish

Night presentation is part of architectural completion.

Later implementation should provide:
- canopy downlights;
- restrained entrance facade wash/grazing;
- subtle sign halo/backlighting;
- controlled interior night glow behind glazing;
- restrained crown/service-screen lighting;
- low-level roof lighting consistent with Level 7 authority.

Prohibited:
- full-building neon outlines;
- excessive bloom;
- nightclub/event-venue lighting;
- arbitrary colored facade lighting.

## 14. Building-to-ground contact

The facade cannot appear to float.

Later finish geometry should coordinate:
- entrance apron / landing;
- base/plinth contact;
- threshold transition;
- simple paving differentiation;
- subtle curb/edge/site-contact logic where compatible with existing site/egress authority.

This is not a full landscape/site redesign.

## 15. Geometric finish quality

The finish pass should intentionally create light-catching geometry:
- small bevels/chamfers where tooling permits;
- reveals;
- real trim thickness;
- panel offsets;
- shadow gaps;
- cap geometry;
- sill/head projections;
- layered portal geometry.

Detail must be organized, not random clutter.
