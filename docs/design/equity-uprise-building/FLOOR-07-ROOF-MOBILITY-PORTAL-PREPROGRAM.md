## 1. Owner intent

The building now has **seven navigable levels**:

1. Floor 1 — Lobby + Intake
2. Floor 2 — Public Forum
3. Floor 3 — Fellowship + Network
4. Floor 4 — Media + Culture
5. Floor 5 — Policy + Proof
6. Floor 6 — Penthouse Command
7. **Level 7 — Roof / Mobility Portal**

Level 7 is the roof, not a normal enclosed occupied floor.

Its digital purpose is to act as the transition plane between the Equity Uprise website and other websites/buildings in the larger McCluster universe.

The canonical interaction concept is:
- user reaches the roof from inside the Equity Uprise building;
- roof presents an overlook/city scene;
- a helicopter/aircraft mobility interaction represents departure;
- selecting another destination transitions/flys the user from Equity Uprise to another website represented as another building/location;
- the roof therefore becomes the cross-site navigation hub.

The cross-site destinations are dynamic and should not be permanently baked into rooftop geometry.

## 2. Physical-world interpretation

The roof should be designed as a plausible real rooftop first, with a digital mobility metaphor layered on top.

Conceptual elements:
- enclosed vertical-core arrival / roof-access point;
- protected stair access;
- roof service/mechanical zones screened from public experience;
- guarded/parapet-protected overlook area;
- modest rooftop observation area;
- a **candidate aviation landing zone / helipad area** if later feasibility work supports it.

The project must not claim that a real rooftop helipad is permitted or buildable until the final site, aircraft type, structural loads, local land-use rules, fire/life-safety requirements, aviation requirements, approach/departure paths, wind, noise and operational constraints are professionally evaluated.

Until that work exists, the canonical term is:

**Candidate Rooftop Mobility Pad / Helipad Zone**

Do not lock exact helipad diameter, aircraft class, approach/departure surface, fuel system, lighting, markings or operating conditions yet.

## 3. Building-wide consequences

Floor 6 may not be designed as a dead-end top floor.

It must preserve:
- continuation of the vertical circulation/core to the roof;
- protected roof access from the building interior;
- structural/service coordination for roof loads;
- MEP/riser termination and rooftop equipment coordination;
- a logical transition from Penthouse Command to Level 7;
- no geometry that would make future roof access impossible.

The elevator may serve Level 7 if final code/engineering/operations permit. If not, the design must still preserve a compliant stair-based roof-access strategy. Final elevator overrun, machine-room/controls, fire-service and roof-door conditions are professional-design items.

## 4. Exterior-master consequences

Level 7 is where the building exterior becomes fully legible.

The final exterior master must coordinate:
- the 72' × 72' building footprint established below;
- the six occupied levels;
- the roof/parapet/crown;
- fixed elevator/stair/service core;
- rooftop access enclosure;
- rooftop mechanical equipment and screening;
- candidate mobility/helipad zone;
- city/context skyline;
- roof-edge/parapet/guard conditions;
- exterior facade/window rhythm established by the lower floors.

The roof scene is not allowed to invent a different building shell from the one defined by Floors 1–6.

## 5. Digital navigation role

Level 7 is the canonical **cross-site mobility portal**.

The helicopter/aircraft is a navigation metaphor. Potential destination objects may represent:
- other McCluster-owned websites;
- separate project websites;
- other branded buildings/locations;
- future external destinations approved by the owner.

The destination registry should eventually be data-driven, for example:
- destination id;
- site/domain;
- display name;
- destination building/location;
- transition asset;
- access requirement;
- return route.

Do not hard-code the site network into a single image.

## 6. Rooftop experience

The roof should feel:
- open-air;
- elevated;
- cinematic but plausible;
- less cluttered than an entertainment rooftop;
- connected to the same blackened steel / gunmetal / stone material family;
- capable of overlooking a city or contextual skyline.

Potential non-aviation elements:
- modest overlook zone;
- low-profile seating only if it does not conflict with safety/operations;
- architectural lighting;
- rooftop wayfinding / destination interface;
- screened mechanical/service zone;
- roof-access enclosure.

Avoid:
- rooftop nightclub;
- pool;
- bar;
- garden covering the aviation zone;
- huge billboard forest;
- multiple unrelated aircraft;
- fantasy runway on a 72' × 72' roof.

## 7. Runway vs helipad

A conventional runway is **not** the intended default for this roof.

The design direction is:
- **helicopter / vertical-lift mobility**, represented by a candidate helipad or future-compatible vertical-lift zone;
- not a fixed-wing runway.

If future eVTOL/VTOL operations become part of the project, they require their own feasibility and regulatory/engineering review.
