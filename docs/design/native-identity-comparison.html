HERE · identity review, round two

The app, put back in the house's own materials.

The first native pass was rejected, correctly: it carried the engineering forward and dropped the identity that makes HERE not read as a template. This round starts from the real production source — album.html and css/style.css at commit 067cc59 — and reads the actual CSS rather than a description of it: the metal ruby gradient, the floating glass panels, the ambient pulse-tinted wash, the crisp-card-in-a-blurred-room staging. Every image below is a real screenshot — the left column served from that commit in a clean worktree, the right column the React Native app rendered through react-native-web and captured in the same browser at the same width.

Two screens, as asked: Album/Home and Now Playing. Films, Catalog, License and Desk are untouched in this pass and still carry the first pass's flatter treatment — they are next, once this direction is confirmed.

Album / Home

Top of screen: the M mark, the curated rail, the full-bleed cover, the metal Play pill.

Originalalbum.html · commit 067cc59   Newapp/(tabs)/index.tsx

Preserved

The M mark top-left, in the same spot. “Curated by the house” as a real horizontal rail of the actual catalog (six other releases from data/albums.json, not invented cards). The Anton display face at hero scale for the album name. The metal ruby gradient on Play — four real stops, a specular top edge, a tinted glow beneath it — not a flat ruby rectangle.

Enhanced

The cover runs full-bleed at phone width instead of the ~150px thumbnail the desktop layout uses. The brand library calls this “the opening field, not a thumbnail beside a shelf” — that was never actually built on web (album.html still uses the small thumbnail today). A phone is the single-purpose canvas that ambition describes, so this is where it gets built, in the site's own gradient/glass/glow materials rather than a generic full-bleed hero.

Known gap

Rail cards for the five other releases are not yet playable (only I AM HERE has real track data ported). Per-row track thumbnails and heart/share icons from the original list (visible in the row comparison below) are not yet in the native rows — noted, not silently dropped.

The playing row

The original comment says it plainly: “the playing row becomes a glowing card in the career's color.” That state, forced on in both, for a fair comparison.

What matches

The left-rail glow and the tinted background wash on the active row, in the track's own pulse color rather than a fixed ruby. Below it, the floating deckbar: rounded glass card, a thin pulse-colored seek rail riding its top edge exactly as .deckbar__seek does, a 40px metal circular play button, and the track's stage name in its accent color where the original shows a live lyric line in the same slot — that lyric line is not wired yet (it needs the timed-lyrics engine from films.html, which is separate, tracked work, not part of this pass).

Now Playing

This screen did not exist at the reference commit — only the deckbar (above) and, separately, films.html's per-track cinematic world. It is the translation that connects them: the deckbar's exact materials at full-screen scale, staged inside the same ambient “room” wash that sits behind every songpage.

Closest original analogfilms.html · the per-track cinematic viewer   Newapp/track/[slug].tsx

Preserved

The blurred, darkened, full-screen photograph behind everything — ported from body.songpage's ambient wash and the .room layer's blur(26px) saturate(1.15) brightness(.6) treatment, approximated here with a heavy blur on the same still plus two pulse-tinted glows and a vignette (src/Room.tsx), since RN has no radial-gradient primitive to copy the CSS directly. The Anton title now carries the track's own pulse color as a genuine glow, not a flat color change.

Enhanced

The photograph itself sits as a crisp floating card over the blurred room, rather than being one flat edge-to-edge rectangle — the same crisp-object-in-a-blurred-field relationship .alb__art has against its own backdrop, applied at the scale a dedicated screen allows. The transport controls got the same metal treatment as the hero Play button, at 72px instead of 40px, since this is the one screen where playback is the entire job.

Known gap

The per-track cinematic world — films.html's multi-scene cutting and timed lyric captions — is not in this screen. That is real, separate engineering (a timed-lyrics player synced to the audio clock, ported from data/lyrics/*.json), flagged as its own follow-up rather than folded into this identity pass.
