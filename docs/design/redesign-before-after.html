Front door

index.html

Mobbin reference

Unseen Studio, Shade, Riverside — homepage heroes

Every reference gives the opening screen exactly one job: one kicker, one headline, one primary action. Unseen Studio carries a three-item nav and a single "View our work". Shade pairs one filled button with a quiet text link rather than two equal buttons.

What changed

  • The duplicate scroll instruction is gone. "Scroll slow, homie" sat bottom-left and "Scroll slow" bottom-right at the same time — one instruction printed twice. The right slot now carries the orbit readout, the only thing over there that says something new.
  • One sound prompt instead of two. The volume card and the Sound pill’s pulsing "Tap for sound" hint were competing for the same single action in the same corner; the pill’s hint now stands down while the card is up and returns when it goes.
  • The floating "Deal with me" pill no longer argues with the hero’s own two doors. It holds while the opening owns the screen and arrives as the hero leaves, so it is still there for the rest of the page.

What was kept

The portrait, the display lockup, the two hero doors and their existing hierarchy — the lead door was already filled ruby against a glass secondary, which is the hierarchy the references call for.

Sales

hire.html

Mobbin reference

Zendesk / Circle / monday.com / Dovetail pricing grids — used as a counter-reference

Four or five visually identical columns with a "Most popular" flag clipped over one of them. Circle badges two of its three plans, which is proof the badge carries no information. Seven configurations rendered this way would be that failure squared.

What changed

  • The rate card became four service chapters. Runway leads on width and type scale because it is where you start, not because a badge says so. There is no "recommended" badge anywhere on the page.
  • The mode switch moved inside the top three services, so seven configurations read as seven states of four chapters rather than seven cards to compare.
  • The old Web & social group — Hosting $87, Web Management $309, Management + Social $875 — is gone. It was the same money the offer ladder was already quoting, printed twice on one site.
  • Every figure now comes from data/offers.json. Nothing is priced in markup, and the legacy plan schema in the page’s structured data was replaced.

What was kept

The published-price principle, the kit line, the financing line, the Teardown as the entry product, and the shoppable package tray — which was rewired to the new rate lines rather than dropped.

Sales

sites.html

Mobbin reference

Contra commission comparison; Stripe cost estimator

Contra puts the client’s own revenue at the top and prints both outcomes side by side, with the amount given up in the largest type — the honesty is the persuasion. Stripe keeps the band arithmetic visible beneath the derived total.

What changed

  • The three-lane $87 / $309 / $875 selector is gone. It was a second pricing system on a page that already carried the offer ladder.
  • Runway prints its arithmetic, not just its total: $30 domain + $30 first month = $60, then the first year resolves to $390 with the working shown. Month one is counted once.
  • Anti-Social renders its approved $875, and Equity Uprise renders $437.50 with the 50% share, the 12-month term and the $105,000 cap stated next to the price rather than in a footnote.
  • The development audit is off the page. Copy like "main carries Web Management at $309/mo AND Management + Social at $875/mo" and every pending-approval chip was removed from the render path.

What was kept

The founding seats, the showcase, the Designer Kicks proof, the terms and the comparison section. data/sites.json still feeds all of them and remains the legacy price record.

Sales

onboard.html

Mobbin reference

Squarespace progressive onboarding; Laravel Cloud survey; Productboard workspace

Squarespace asks one question per screen over a NAMED step rail (Site title · Homepage · Pages · Colors · Fonts), and asks the qualifying questions before demanding an account. Productboard’s completion state is the workspace itself, already populated, with the first task ticked because signing up completed it.

What changed

  • One primary question per screen, over a named rail rather than a bare percentage bar.
  • Four service paths from one engine. Runway skips mode selection entirely because it has no Equity version; Equity Uprise is never preselected anywhere.
  • Answers autosave and the flow resumes on the exact question it left. Back navigation preserves what was typed.
  • Equity Uprise gets its own acknowledgement screen stating the price, the share, the term and the cap, and it blocks until ticked.
  • The end state is a workspace — person, organization, service, property, roles — with 2 of 5 setup steps already struck through, not a thank-you page.

What was kept

The person-account-to-organization-workspace chain, the Supabase submission, the email fallback, and the legacy ?plan= links, which still resolve onto the new offers.
