No script. Beats only. Everybody plays themselves real names, real you. Read your parts, know the beats, say it whatever you'd actually say. Notes are marked plain:


THE 5 RULES read out loud together before day one


1. Play everything dead serious. Nobody on camera thinks anything is funny. If you laugh, the joke dies.
2. Let silence sit. Don't rush to fill it. The pause IS the joke.
3. The doc camera catches what you're hiding. You get two looks at the lens all season: "you seeing this?" and "you ain't see that." That's it.
4. Whatever's wild, treat it like it's normal. Whatever's normal, treat it like it's wild.
5. If something happens once, it happens three times. Every bit comes back.

## RULES WE DON'T BREAK


- The HR jokes aim at the COMPANY, not at the complaint. The company is too scared to ever check anything — that fear is the punchline, and it's what keeps all three employed. Laire half believes himself every time. Never play it as a full con. The show never rules on it.
- Matthew is never dumb. Not one frame. Smartest man in the building, spending all of it on the wrong thing.
- Tanuj is never scary. Sweetest man on screen. The scary part arrives after he's gone.
- Blow-ups have no build. Instant. And after, Laire is fine and the ROOM is wrecked.
- Things we never show or explain: the raccoon. The seven-fifty. Who bit the donut. The full clean bodega track. The screening screen. What's on Tanuj's laptop. **The itch is the product.**
- The wildest ideas — running off the replacement hires, all that — hold it for mid-season, after people trust the show. The pilot stays the most grounded episode so the crazy ones hit harder.
- Every episode: one client, three departments, three disasters, one cover-up. And the work is always undeniable. That's the deal with the audience.
