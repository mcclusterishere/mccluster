# PART 0: THE ONE DISTINCTION THAT DECIDES EVERYTHING

Every agency on this list sorts your work into one of two buckets. Get this
wrong and you will get rejected, or worse, sued.

## COMMERCIAL (Royalty-Free / "RF")

**What it is:** a buyer can put it on a billboard, a product box, an ad.

**What it requires:**
- **Model release** signed by every identifiable person
- **Property release** for recognisable private property, some buildings,
  artwork, and branded interiors
- **No logos, no brand names, no trademarks, no recognisable art on walls**
- No newsworthy specificity. It needs to be *generic* enough to illustrate
  an idea

**What it pays:** more per licence, sells far more often, and it is the
whole microstock market.

**Your reality:** almost nothing you have shot so far qualifies. The Candler
groundbreaking has a Publix logo, a branded banner, and nine identifiable
officials. Dressed for Success has minors, Dillard's branding and Polo bags.
**Neither can be sold commercially, ever, without paper you do not have.**

## EDITORIAL

**What it is:** licensed to illustrate news, education and commentary. A
magazine, a newspaper, a documentary, a textbook.

**What it requires:**
- **NO model release. NO property release.** This is the point.
- **Truth.** The caption must be factually accurate and you may not
  meaningfully retouch. Cropping and exposure are fine; removing a bystander
  is fraud.
- A caption in strict wire format (below)
- Genuine news, public event, or documentary value

**What it pays:** less volume, higher price per sale, and a *long* tail:
archives sell for decades.

**Your reality:** **this is your entire body of work.** A county
groundbreaking with named officials, a civic rally, a mentoring programme at
a mall, a Juneteenth festival. You are already an editorial photographer;
you just haven't been paid like one.

### The caption format that gets accepted everywhere

Wire agencies reject on caption format more than on picture quality. Learn
this one line and you clear most of the bar:

```
CITY, STATE, UNITED STATES - MONTH DD: Description of what is happening,
naming people left to right with titles, at Venue in City, State on
Month DD, YYYY. (Photo by Matthew McCluster/McCluster Corp)
```

Real one from your own work:

```
DECATUR, GEORGIA, UNITED STATES - JUNE 11: DeKalb County CEO Lorraine
Cochran-Johnson, District 3 Commissioner Nicole Massiah and District 7
Commissioner Dr. LaDena Bolton break ground on the $28.9 million Candler
Crossing development at Candler Road and Glenwood Road in Decatur, Georgia
on June 11, 2026. (Photo by Matthew McCluster/McCluster Corp)
```

**The Vault already has every field in that sentence.** Generating captions
in this exact format is a small addition to the pipeline.

### ⚠ On exclusivity: read this before you tick a box

Getty/iStock **exclusive** pays more per sale but you **cannot license the
same file anywhere else**. For someone building 26 non-exclusive lanes,
exclusivity is almost always the wrong trade. **Stay non-exclusive.**

# PART 4: THE AGGREGATORS
### (one upload, they fan it out, and they take a cut for it)

| Service | Takes | Reaches | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Wirestock** | photo + video + AI | Shutterstock, Adobe, Alamy, Dreamstime, Pond5, Depositphotos, 123RF, Freepik, Canva, Getty/iStock, Science Photo Library | **15% of royalties.** ~100k contributors, 3M assets. Genuinely useful for reaching Canva and Freepik, which are hard to enter directly |
| **BlackBox** | **VIDEO ONLY, no photos** | multiple video agencies | 15%. Video lane only |
| **Microstock+ / StockSubmitter / Xpiks** | tools, not agencies | 30+ agencies | **Software you run. They take 0% of royalties.** You keep your own accounts. Strictly better than an aggregator *if* you have the accounts |

**My call:** run your **own accounts** for the big lanes (Adobe,
Shutterstock, Alamy, Dreamstime, Pond5). The Vault can drive those SFTP
drops directly at 0% cut. Use **Wirestock only** to reach Canva/Freepik/
Science Photo Library, which are otherwise closed. Paying 15% forever to
reach Adobe is money set on fire when you already have SFTP credentials.

# PART 5: THE 8-HOUR ONBOARDING DAY

Do it in this order. Have these open before you start:

- **Government photo ID** (passport or driver's licence), needed for
  identity verification at most agencies
- **EIN 394466255** and the McCluster Corp legal name/address: you file as
  a business, not an individual
- **W-9** (US): you'll fill this in on nearly every platform
- **PayPal and/or Payoneer**, the two payout rails everyone supports
- **10 to 20 of your strongest, cleanest frames**: several agencies gate entry
  on an initial quality test

### Hour 1: The money-first pair
1. **Adobe Stock**: contributor.stock.adobe.com → sign up with Adobe ID →
   W-9 → payout (PayPal/Skrill, $25 minimum) → upload 10 test images. Then
   request **SFTP access** in the contributor portal.
2. **Shutterstock**: submit.shutterstock.com → account → ID verification →
   W-9 → 10-image quality test → **request FTP credentials**.

### Hour 2: Alamy, and switch on Live News
3. **Alamy**: alamy.com/contributor → account → tax form → upload 3-image
   QC test → **once passed, enable Live News in your account settings**.
   This is the single highest-value switch on the entire list for you.

### Hour 3: The rest of the FTP tier
4. Dreamstime  5. 123RF  6. Depositphotos  7. Pond5  8. Zoonar
9. PantherMedia  10. ClipDealer

Each is the same loop: account → tax form → payout method → test upload →
**request FTP credentials**. Write every hostname/username/password
straight into your password manager as you go. You will not remember 26 of
them, and the Vault will need them.

### Hour 4: The regional and long-tail
11. Pixta (Japan) 12. MostPhotos 13. ScanStockPhoto 14. StockFresh
15. YayImages 16. Colourbox 17. MotionElements 18. Vecteezy

### Hour 5: Getty/iStock + video
19. **Getty/iStock ESP**: istockphoto.com/workwithus → application →
    portfolio review. **Choose NON-EXCLUSIVE.**
20. Storyblocks, Envato Elements, Motion Array, Videvo

### Hours 6 and 7: The editorial wires (your real lane)
21. ZUMA Press  22. SIPA USA  23. NurPhoto  24. SOPA Images
25. Imago  26. Pacific Press  27. Newscom

Each needs a **portfolio link**. Send them
`matthew.mccluster.org/walls.html`. That is exactly what those wall pages were
built for, and it is a far stronger submission than a Dropbox folder.

### Hour 8: Local outlets + the aggregator
28. Email the photo editors at **Decaturish, Rough Draft Atlanta, On Common
    Ground News, The Atlanta Voice, AJC**. Short: who you are, what you
    shoot, the Candler and Dressed for Success walls, availability for
    assignment. Attach nothing; link everything.
29. **Wirestock** account for Canva/Freepik/Science Photo Library reach.
30. **Stocksy**: check whether applications are open; if not, calendar it.

# THE HONEST FRAME, ONE MORE TIME

Setting up 30 accounts is a real day's work and it is worth doing. It is
worth doing because it turns **work you already got paid for** into
recurring income at near-zero marginal cost, not because microstock is a
living. The benchmark is still about **$1 per image per year**.

The lanes on this list that can actually change your income are, in order:

1. **Local publications** (Part 2, #17): pays now, builds the byline
2. **Alamy Live News** (Part 2, #1): one switch, your existing archive
3. **The editorial wires** (Part 2): the byline compounds
4. **Adobe Stock** (Part 1, #1): the best of the microstock money
5. **Everything else**: a long tail that costs nothing once automated

Do Part 2 first. Part 1 is the background hum.
