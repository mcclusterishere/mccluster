Modeled on Wants and Needs' drop film, *"10 Days To Make My Clothing
Drop Go Viral… Did It Actually Work?"* (youtu.be/vuLfDsdUjLU, May 2026,
104K views on a 5K-subscriber channel). Their format: document the ten

## PART 1: THE PLAYBOOK, EXTRACTED

What Wants and Needs actually runs, format by format:

1. **The countdown documentary.** The 10 days are themselves the
   product: one YouTube film ("will this drop go viral?") cut from
   daily footage, plus a daily short. The suspense IS the marketing.
2. **Skits.** Relatable/funny scenarios worn in-product ("Knew
   something was up 😅"). The clothes are the costume, never the pitch.
3. **The challenge.** "Sweatsuit Challenge: how many can you get?"
   A game built around the product, infinitely remixable.
4. **Street reactions.** Real people react to the pieces on camera:
   third-party proof, clip-able moments.
5. **Drop transitions.** The TikTok clothing staple: stomp/jump,
   instant outfit change into the drop.
6. **Waitlist CTAs.** "002 coming soon. Join the waitlist, link in
   bio." Every video ends at the list, not the store.
7. **Scarcity beats.** "Sweatsuits almost out of stock!" reaction
   content. Urgency as its own video.
8. **The email trade.** 10% off for joining the list, so the audience is
   owned before the drop, not rented on drop day.
9. **Behind the scenes.** Samples arriving, quality checks, packing
   orders. Process as content.
10. **Launch day, live.** Day 10 the drop opens on camera and the
    results are the cliffhanger the whole film was sold on.

## THE McCLUSTER 10 DAYS (each idea, made ours)

Our edge they don't have: broadcast-grade production, an album for a
soundtrack, and a store that already knows its buyers. Every short is
scored by the record. The drop markets the music and the music markets
the drop.

| Day | The move | The format borrowed |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | "I have 10 days to sell out Drop 001": face to camera, samples unboxed on the light table | Countdown doc + BTS |
| 2 | The M-mark reveal: puff-print close-ups, macro b-roll, *Who Did The Shoot* under it | BTS / product film |
| 3 | Skit: "POV: he says have no fear" where the hoodie is the punchline | Skit |
| 4 | **The Runway Walk challenge**: how many McCluster pieces can you wear down a hallway walk, scored by *Runway Walk* | Challenge |
| 5 | Street reactions in Bridgeport: hand people the hoodie, film the read | Street reactions |
| 6 | Drop transition: camera flash, then suited in the full sweatsuit, *Antisocial* hit | Drop transition |
| 7 | Waitlist push: "Drop 001 is 48 hours out. Profile up at the door." CTA is **make a profile** (the store's own list; every profile = name, email, phone) | Waitlist CTA |
| 8 | The wall walk: hang the pieces in the gallery like frames. "The wall sells the work; now it wears it" | Brand world |
| 9 | Scarcity, honest: "There are exactly N of each. When it's gone the drop is a rerun" | Scarcity beat |
| 10 | **Live at the door**: drop goes live on merch.html, react to the first orders on camera, the film's ending writes itself | Launch day |

Where their email list sits, ours is the **profile register**. The
store already gates buying on it, so drop hype compounds into owned
buyers, not just followers. Charity clock note: while the mission fund
runs (through Sept 30), drop revenue speaks as program support like
every other money point.
