# Country Assignment Rules

The 21 core countries must be selected as a campaign set, not one mission at a time.

Country placement is an educational systems-design decision, not scenery selection. Read `TECHNOLOGY-CULTURE-PLACEMENT-DOCTRINE.md` before proposing or canonizing a country.

## Hard constraints

- one country per core arc;
- three distinct broad regions/sites inside that country;
- fictional mission facilities only;
- no inferred character nationality/jurisdiction;
- no live operational data dependency;
- no exact sensitive real facility as a target;
- every assignment carries canon/source references;
- every assignment carries a dated technology/institution adoption profile;
- every assignment defines an implicit cultural-learning plan across the three missions;
- no country may be described or scored as globally "advanced," "behind," or technologically inferior;
- local technology claims must be sourced rather than inferred from continent, ethnicity, income, or stereotype.

## Campaign-level balance

The final 21-country set should avoid accidental over-concentration in one continent unless the character/curriculum canon requires it. Optimize for meaningful global spread, distinct physical environments, distinct urban/rural/infrastructure contexts, representation already present in the character pool, and genuinely different technology/institution ecosystems.

Geographic diversity alone is insufficient. The campaign should expose players to materially different ways that identity, payments, connectivity, infrastructure, transport, enterprise systems, regulation, and everyday technology operate around the world.

## Assignment score

For each candidate core-country pair record a 0–5 score for:

- character/jurisdiction canon fit;
- curriculum/learning fit;
- threat-model and attack-surface fit;
- technology/institution adoption fit;
- cultural-learning opportunity;
- narrative fit;
- availability of three distinct regions;
- environmental/architectural distinctiveness;
- campaign geographic balance;
- source quality and freshness.

Also record blockers, plausible alternative countries, stereotype/red-team notes, and source confidence. The highest numeric score is not automatically canon; scores make the editorial decision auditable.

## Required placement rationale

Before canonization, answer all of these:

1. Why does this lesson become clearer or more memorable in this country?
2. Which locally documented technology, institutional, infrastructure, or threat conditions matter to the mission?
3. What would be educationally lost if the same mission were moved to the United States or another plausible country?
4. How do the three mission regions show internal diversity rather than treating one city as the entire nation?
5. What cultural knowledge will the player absorb through play without being given a trivia lecture?
6. Which details are factual, which are fictionalized, and which remain uncertain?

If these cannot be answered with evidence, the country remains a candidate rather than canon.
