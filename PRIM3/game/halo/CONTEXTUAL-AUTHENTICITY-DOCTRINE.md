# Contextual Authenticity Doctrine

This doctrine governs PRIM3 Halo worldbuilding, mission placement, environment design, technology selection, cultural representation, and educational framing.

## Core rule

Every design choice should emerge from researched context rather than decoration.

A country, region, technology, institution, character behavior, environment, or mission mechanic should be selected because it makes the learning objective more accurate, meaningful, memorable, or narratively coherent.

## Technology context

Do not rank countries or regions on a single advanced-versus-behind scale. Model technology as a multidimensional adoption profile that may vary by sector, institution, geography, income, regulation, infrastructure, and time.

Examples of dimensions that may matter:

- physical SIM versus eSIM prevalence;
- mobile-first versus fixed-broadband usage;
- mobile-money and payment-network usage;
- cloud versus on-premises infrastructure;
- device age and repair ecosystems;
- identity and KYC systems;
- QR and contactless payment adoption;
- industrial and operational technology;
- public transit and ticketing systems;
- government digital services;
- power and network reliability;
- data-center and backbone infrastructure;
- carrier, banking, and regulatory practices.

A mission may use these differences only when they are supported by current evidence and materially improve the lesson.

## Attack-surface fit

Place a mission where the real local technology and institutional context makes its attack surface or defense problem pedagogically meaningful.

Do not treat one technical artifact as the entire threat model. For example, SIM-swap and number-takeover fraud are not limited to physical SIM cards. eSIM environments may still be vulnerable through carrier-account compromise, fraudulent reprovisioning, weak identity recovery, insider abuse, or related processes.

The placement question is not "can this happen here?" but "does this context make the mechanism easier to understand, more realistic, or more educational than another plausible location?"

## Cultural learning

Cultural learning should usually be implicit and environmental rather than delivered as trivia.

Useful channels include:

- language and signage;
- architecture and built environment;
- geography and climate;
- transportation patterns;
- music and sound design;
- visual design and material culture;
- institutions and public services;
- commerce and everyday digital behavior;
- food and social spaces when narratively relevant;
- historically grounded local context;
- regionally plausible infrastructure.

The player should absorb cultural literacy while pursuing the mission objective.

## Internal diversity

No city, region, or demographic may stand in for an entire country.

The three missions assigned to one country should expose materially different regional, social, geographic, infrastructural, or institutional contexts wherever the evidence supports that distinction.

## Representation safeguards

Do not use nationality, ethnicity, religion, poverty, language, or regional identity as a shortcut for intelligence, morality, technical competence, criminality, violence, corruption, or sophistication.

Avoid stereotype mechanics, exoticism, poverty tourism, continent-level generalizations, and invented cultural claims.

Characters remain individuals. Local context shapes the environment and institutions, not a deterministic personality template.

## Evidence standard

Country and region placement decisions must carry:

- dated sources;
- source confidence;
- technology and institutional context;
- attack-surface or curriculum fit;
- cultural-learning opportunity;
- alternatives considered;
- a written placement rationale;
- any uncertainty or contested assumptions.

Sources should be refreshed when the mission depends on technology adoption or regulation that can change materially over time.

## Design stack

Every core mission arc should be evaluated across five layers:

1. primary technical competency;
2. operational judgment and problem solving;
3. local technology and institutional systems;
4. cultural and geographic literacy;
5. narrative and character fit.

The best placement is where these layers reinforce one another rather than compete.

## Canon threshold

Research candidates are not canon merely because they are plausible.

A location becomes canon only after the evidence record, curriculum fit, cultural treatment, and narrative fit survive review. Unknowns should remain explicitly unknown rather than being filled with convenient assumptions.

## Scope

This doctrine applies to all future PRIM3 Halo country assignments, mission variants, fictional facilities, environmental storytelling, character deployment decisions, technology choices, and worldbuilding passes unless a later canon decision explicitly supersedes it.
