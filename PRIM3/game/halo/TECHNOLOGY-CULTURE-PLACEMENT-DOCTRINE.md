# Technology and Cultural Mission Placement Doctrine

This doctrine governs how PRIM3 Halo chooses countries, regions, technologies, environments, and cultural context for missions.

## 1. Technology context is not a hierarchy

PRIM3 must not classify a country or region as simply "advanced" or "behind." Technology adoption is uneven and domain-specific. A place can lead in one area while relying on older or different systems in another because of economics, regulation, infrastructure, device mix, market structure, geography, or user behavior.

Mission placement therefore uses an **adoption profile**, not a development ranking.

Relevant dimensions may include:

- physical SIM, eSIM, and multi-SIM prevalence;
- prepaid versus postpaid mobile usage;
- mobile money, bank, card, cash, and real-time payment rails;
- identity and SIM/number registration rules;
- dominant handset and device classes;
- 2G/3G/4G/5G availability and actual usage;
- fixed broadband versus mobile-first access;
- cloud versus on-premise enterprise infrastructure;
- authentication norms and account-recovery practices;
- public power/connectivity reliability;
- transport, logistics, public-sector, industrial, and communications infrastructure;
- common fraud patterns documented by local regulators or credible research.

No mission may infer a technology profile from continent, ethnicity, income level, appearance, or stereotype.

## 2. Attack-surface fit is a country-assignment criterion

A country should host a mission when the local technology and institutional environment makes the lesson unusually legible, plausible, or educational.

Examples:

- a mobile-money-heavy environment may make identity takeover, SIM/number fraud, account recovery, and social engineering especially meaningful;
- a highly cashless/contactless environment may better support payment-token, NFC, wallet, or point-of-sale lessons;
- a region with major maritime logistics may support port/OT/ICS scenarios;
- a hyperscale/cloud corridor may support cloud identity, data-center, or supply-chain scenarios;
- a mobile-first region may teach different availability, authentication, and device-management tradeoffs than a fixed-broadband-heavy region.

Placement must be backed by dated sources. The game should model the **local attack surface**, not merely use the country as scenery.

## 3. SIM-swap canon rule

Physical SIM cards are not the sole prerequisite for SIM-swap or number-takeover fraud. eSIM environments can still be attacked through carrier-account takeover, fraudulent reprovisioning, weak recovery procedures, compromised identity checks, or insider abuse.

Therefore PRIM3 must not encode the false rule "physical SIM = vulnerable, eSIM = safe." The educational target is the identity, carrier, provisioning, recovery, and social-engineering chain.

A physical-SIM-heavy or mobile-money-heavy market may still be a stronger narrative fit when regulators document SIM registration, SIM-swap fraud, impersonation, and account takeover as locally important risks.

## 4. Cultural learning is environmental, not a quiz layer

Every country arc should teach cultural and geographic knowledge primarily through world design, character behavior, sound, architecture, infrastructure, language, institutions, and mission constraints rather than trivia popups.

A three-mission country arc should expose at least three distinct lenses on the country. Candidate lenses include:

- language and signage;
- architecture and urban morphology;
- regional geography and climate;
- transportation and street/public-space patterns;
- food, clothing, craft, visual art, music, or material culture where narratively appropriate;
- local institutions, commerce, public services, and communications norms;
- historically important infrastructure or economic systems;
- technology adoption and everyday digital behavior.

The three missions should also show internal diversity. One city, neighborhood, accent, religion, class, or region must never stand in for the entire country.

## 5. Representation guardrails

- Real countries and broad real geography are permitted; mission facilities and hostile operations remain fictional.
- Cultural details require sources or reviewed subject-matter input.
- Avoid exoticism, poverty tourism, caricature, or assigning moral/technical traits by nationality.
- Local people are not environmental props. They should have agency, occupations, expertise, disagreements, and ordinary life beyond the mission.
- A country's technological constraints must be framed as system conditions and tradeoffs, not intelligence or competence deficits.
- If evidence conflicts or is stale, mark the profile uncertain and do not canonize the placement yet.

## 6. Country-arc evidence gate

Before a country becomes canon for a core arc, the assignment record must contain:

1. curriculum fit;
2. character/canon fit where applicable;
3. technology-adoption profile;
4. documented threat or operational relevance;
5. three-region diversity plan;
6. cultural-learning plan;
7. source dates and confidence;
8. stereotype/red-team review;
9. explanation of why this country is a better teaching environment than plausible alternatives.

A country is not selected merely because a mission could happen there. It should earn the assignment because the location makes the learning better.

## 7. Ghana social-engineering candidate

Ghana is a strong **candidate for research**, not yet a locked country assignment, for a social-engineering / mobile-identity / account-recovery arc.

Why it merits consideration:

- Ghana's communications regulator continues to operate a formal SIM/number registration regime linked to verified identity and announced a new 2026 registration effort to strengthen identity validation and fraud controls.
- Ghana's central bank has specifically identified SIM-swap attacks, impersonation, unauthorized account access, and fraud around the expanding electronic/mobile-money ecosystem as relevant risks.
- This creates a locally grounded setting for teaching that the real weakness is often the human and identity-recovery chain around the telecom/payment system.

The final mission should not teach that Ghana is "less advanced" or that SIM fraud is unique to Ghana. It should teach how a particular adoption and regulatory environment changes the attack surface.

## 8. Design consequence

Country selection happens after curriculum and context research. The canonical sequence is:

`learning objective -> threat model -> technology/institution profile -> cultural/geographic opportunity -> character fit -> country -> three distinct regions -> fictional facilities -> mission variants`

This ordering is mandatory for future country assignment work.
